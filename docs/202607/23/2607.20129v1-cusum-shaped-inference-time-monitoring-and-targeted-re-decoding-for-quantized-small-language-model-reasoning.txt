# Abstract 

Quantized small autoregressive reasoning models can enter long, repetitive, or otherwise unproductive trajectories, yet inference-time compute is usually allocated without observing how a trajectory develops. Building on an earlier token-level e-CUSUM controller, we develop MGT-B ( Monitoring-Guided Test-time Backtracking), a substantially revised external controller that maps overlapping windows of pre-sampling uncertainty and degeneration features to position-conditional empirical tail probabilities, accumulates mixture betting factors with a CUSUM-shaped reset, and responds to an alarm by estimating a rollback point, restoring token and key–value-cache state, and performing constrained re-decoding. To audit whether the effect persists on problem identities first observed after the manual choice of log threshold h = 10 , we retrospectively exclude 260 IDs present in pre-threshold artifacts and retain the chronologically first post-threshold pair for each remaining ID, yielding a 240-pair chronology-audit set. On this set, exact-normalized accuracy changes from 82/240 to 88/240 (+2.50 percentage points; 13 corrections, 7 regressions; exact McNemar p = 0 .2632 ;paired bootstrap 95% interval [−1.25 , +6 .25] points). A broader 467-pair historical-coverage set of seed-matched pairs changes accuracy from 146/467 to 167/467 (+4.50 points; exact McNemar p = 0 .000753 ; paired bootstrap 95% interval [+1 .93 , +7 .07] ), but it includes 200 seed-1 IDs available before or during manual threshold selection and is therefore reported only as an exploratory coverage estimate. Operationally, all 316 no-alarm outputs in the 467-pair historical-coverage set are identical to vanilla, while the 151 alarmed trajectories contain 29 corrections and 8 regressions. Neither accuracy analysis is treated as confirmatory: the 240-pair chronology-audit set is reconstructed retrospectively from file artifacts, and the nominally small p-value of the 467-pair historical-coverage set is not independent of threshold selection. The empirical factors are also not established as a valid e-process or e-detector. The results support a selective monitoring-and-repair mechanism for the studied MATH-500 setting, rather than a general or theoretically certified reasoning improvement. 1arXiv:2607.20129v1 [cs.AI] 22 Jul 2026 1 Introduction 

Distilled reasoning models make long chain-of-thought generation feasible on limited hardware, while low-bit post-training quantization further reduces deployment cost. This efficiency can be accompanied by a disproportionate loss of mathematical reasoning quality, longer trajectories, and errors that emerge early and propagate through later steps (Li et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Lotfi et al., 2026). These observations motivate an intervention point inside decoding: if an unproductive trajectory can be recognized while it is still unfolding, compute can be redirected before the model exhausts its budget or commits to an unrecoverable suffix. Autoregressive reasoning is path dependent. A locally plausible token changes every subsequent conditional distribution, and a small model may spend thousands of tokens in repetition, self-reinforcing detours, or continued deliberation that never yields an extractable answer. Repetitive degeneration is well documented in neural generation (Holtzman et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023), and recent analyses identify circular, self-reinforcing loops as a distinct failure mode of long reasoning trajectories (Duan et al., 2026). The relevant monitoring question is therefore not whether one token is individually surprising, but whether a recent segment provides accumulating evidence that the trajectory has changed and whether a targeted intervention can still recover it. A tempting starting point is the centered chosen-token log-probability. If Wt is sampled from the same distribution pt used to compute entropy Ht, then 

Dt = log pt(Wt) + Ht (1) has conditional mean zero. This gives a mathematically valid martingale increment under the model’s own sampling law, but it measures sampling self-consistency rather than reasoning health. In a confident repetition loop, both log pt(Wt) and Ht are close to zero, so the increment can remain silent precisely when an explicit degeneration signal is needed. This negative design result, developed in an earlier preprint (Anonymous, 2026), motivates combining uncertainty with direct repetition and local-change features rather than treating centered token log-probability as a standalone alarm. We study a fixed, weight-preserving decoding controller, MGT-B, that implements this principle. Figure 1 summarizes the loop. At each token, the controller records pre-sampling entropy and the sampled token’s log-probability. It aggregates these quantities with repeated n-gram, confident-repetition, and local entropy-change features over overlapping windows; maps the resulting score to an empirical upper-tail probability conditional on position; and applies a mixture betting transform. A CUSUM-shaped recurrence reduces the influence of an uninformative prefix. When the statistic crosses a threshold, the controller estimates a rollback point from the reset history, truncates all coupled state, and re-decodes with a lower temperature, a repetition penalty, and constraints against suspect n-grams. The statistical terminology requires care. Classical CUSUM originates in sequential change detection (Page, 1954). E-values and e-processes support optional-stopping-safe inference only under appropriate conditional validity conditions (Shafer, 2021; Ramdas et al., 2023), while e-detectors provide restart-valid change-detection constructions when their assumptions hold (Shin et al., 2024). The empirical tail probabilities in the present controller are computed from overlapping, dependent language-model windows and reused calibration data. They are not shown to be conditionally super-uniform. We therefore call the implementation CUSUM-shaped 

or e-CUSUM-inspired , and treat its threshold as an empirical operating point rather than a theoretical false-alarm certificate. 

Relation to the prior preprint. An earlier anonymized preprint introduced a token-level uncertainty– repetition score, a global healthy baseline, a linear betting process, and a simpler intervention policy (Anonymous, 2026). The present manuscript changes the monitoring granularity, calibration, betting construction, rollback logic, and decoding intervention: it uses six overlapping-window features, position-conditional empirical calibration, a beta-mixture transform, reset-history-based rollback estimation, full restoration of coupled controller state, and marker-free constrained re-decoding. It also replaces the earlier pilot protocol with a larger paired MATH-500 study and explicit token accounting. The larger 467-pair historical-coverage set yields a stronger nominal contrast, but it is contaminated by manual threshold selection and is not interpreted as independent significance. On that exploratory set, the evaluated control policies receive slightly larger permitted per-item budgets than MGT-B, while MGT-B attains the highest aggregate accuracy among them. The contrast with random and periodic rollback provides exploratory policy-level evidence that the observed gain is not explained by backtracking alone. The current evidence does not yet identify which elements of the complete pipeline—the feature construction, sequential accumulation, rollback localization, or constrained re-decoding—produce this advantage, nor does it support a cross-model or cross-dataset reasoning claim. 

# 2 Related Work 

Quantization and reasoning trajectories. Low-bit quantization can degrade mathematical reasoning more sharply than ordinary language modeling, with reported increases in chain length, execution errors, and early errors that propagate through later reasoning (Li et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025). Recent work further argues that quantized reasoners often continue deliberating after reaching a viable intermediate answer, and 3can overproduce branch-opening markers such as “wait” or “but” (Lotfi et al., 2026). These findings motivate monitoring the trajectory during generation rather than only reranking completed outputs. Our experiments focus on a 4-bit 1.5B reasoning model, so quantized reasoning is the empirical scope of the current paper rather than a claim about all language models. 

Confidence- and entropy-based online control. Token probabilities, entropy, and internal activations are widely used as uncertainty signals. Token-level uncertainty has been aggregated to assess factual reliability after generation (Fadeeva et al., 2024), while DeepConf uses confidence to filter completed reasoning traces before voting (Fu et al., 2025). More directly, reasoning-path deviation monitoring uses local entropy behavior to detect overthinking and terminate a reasoning block online (Guan et al., 2026); related work connects entropy dynamics to underthinking and reasoning quality (Wang et al., 2025; Agarwal et al., 2025). Our controller differs by combining uncertainty with explicit repetition and local-change features, accumulating evidence across windows, and responding with rollback and re-decoding rather than only filtering or early termination. The random and periodic rollback controls support the value of selective intervention at the policy level; simple entropy-only and entropy-ratio variants remain useful for attributing that advantage to specific signal or accumulation components. 

Degeneration, repetition, and circular reasoning. Neural text degeneration can be self-reinforcing, arising from both training-data repetition and decoding dynamics (Holtzman et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023). In long reasoning models, circular reasoning can emerge as a state collapse in which semantic repetition precedes overt textual loops; notably, Duan et al. (2026) use CUSUM for early loop prediction. This is direct prior art for CUSUM-based monitoring of reasoning degeneration. The present work differs in the monitored feature set, empirical positional calibration, state-consistent rollback, and final-answer evaluation, but does not claim that CUSUM itself or its use for loop prediction is novel. The broader lesson is that low entropy is ambiguous: it may indicate either confident progress or a confident loop, which is why our score includes direct repetition and confidence-increase terms. 

Test-time scaling, backtracking, and self-correction. Self-consistency spends additional inference compute on independent paths and selects by agreement (Wang et al., 2023); adaptive test-time scaling reallocates compute according to problem difficulty (Snell et al., 2024). Tree of Thoughts searches and backtracks among explicit reasoning states (Yao et al., 2023), Self-Refine iterates feedback and revision (Madaan et al., 2023), and self-backtracking methods train or prompt models to decide when and where to reverse course (Yang et al., 2025). Work on self-correction also shows that generic revision is a substantive alternative rather than a negligible control (Tsui, 2025). MGT-B instead follows one token trajectory until a training-free sequential signal activates, then restores an earlier token and KV-cache state and locally re-decodes. A fair evaluation must therefore compare against restarts, self-correction, random or periodic intervention, and multi-path sampling under a transparent common budget rule while reporting both permitted and realized compute. 

Process supervision and intermediate verification. Process supervision trains verifiers to score intermediate reasoning steps rather than only final answers (Lightman et al., 2024). Our controller also acts before completion, but it uses neither step labels nor a learned verifier. Its alarms identify deviation relative to an empirical reference, not semantic incorrectness, and therefore cannot inherit claims about process-level correctness. 

Anytime-valid monitoring and sequential change detection. CUSUM accumulates evidence for distributional change while reducing the influence of a long pre-change prefix (Page, 1954). Betting-based tests, e-values, and e-processes can provide time-uniform guarantees under appropriate conditional constraints (Ville, 1939; Shafer, 2021; Ramdas et al., 2023); conformal test martingales and adaptive conformal betting instantiate related ideas for sequential monitoring (Vovk et al., 2005; Podkopaev et al., 2024). WATCH applies weighted-conformal martingales to monitor deployed models across observations (Prinster et al., 2025), while e-detectors combine restarted e-processes for change detection (Shin et al., 2024). Our setting instead monitors dependent, overlapping windows within one autoregressive trajectory. The empirical calibration 4used here does not establish the conditional validity required by those frameworks, so the recurrence is evaluated as an operational CUSUM-shaped statistic rather than an anytime-valid test. 

# 3 Problem Setup 

Let x be a prompt and let Wt ∈ V denote the token sampled at generation step t. Before sampling, the language model produces 

pt(v) = P(Wt = v | F t−1), v ∈ V , (2) where Ft−1 contains the prompt, retained token prefix, model cache, monitor state, previous alarms, and decoding decisions. The controller observes the pre-sampling logits corresponding to pt and the realized Wt;it does not observe whether the current reasoning is semantically correct. Let Bj be the jth token window, with window length L and stride r. The monitor produces a feature vector 

Zj ∈ R6 and scalar score sj = w⊤Zj . A calibration procedure maps (sj , end (Bj )) to an empirical upper-tail probability qj . The sequential detector then produces an incremental nonnegative factor ej , a reset statistic 

Sj , and an alarm time 

τ = inf {j ≥ 1 : Sj ≥ h}, (3) where h is a log-scale threshold. An intervention policy π maps the alarm and retained state to a rollback position and new decoding parameters. We distinguish four token quantities. Sampled tokens count every decoding event, including abandoned branches. Emitted tokens are retained in the final completion. Deleted tokens are sampled and later removed by rollback. Injected tokens , if any, are controller-authored prompt tokens; the experiments analyzed here disable injection. This accounting prevents an accuracy change from being presented independently of its inference cost. The primary endpoint is exact-normalized final-answer accuracy. Secondary operational endpoints are alarm frequency, intervention frequency, corrections (vanilla wrong, controlled correct), regressions (vanilla correct, controlled wrong), extractability, sampled/emitted/deleted tokens, and logged latency. Detector precision, recall, false-alarm rate on a labeled healthy test set, and detection delay require temporal degradation labels or true changepoints; none are available.

4.5 Calibration and alarm threshold 

The automatic procedure replays the statistic over calibration trajectories and searches an exponential threshold grid for the smallest candidate whose observed per-trajectory alarm rate is at most a target. The INT4 artifact selects a threshold of 57 ,709 .073 from 34 retained trajectories and reports one alarm, or 

2.94% . Because the same trajectories form the empirical score pools and select the threshold, this is an in-sample fit, not independent false-alarm validation. A manually lowered log threshold h = 10 , equivalent to 

22 ,026 .466 on the original scale, was then written to threshold_log10.json ; replaying it on the calibration trajectories alarms on 3/34, or 8.82% . Artifact chronology places this manual-threshold file after the seed-1 automatic-threshold run and before the subsequent manual-threshold evaluation artifacts. These timestamps support the retrospective chronology audit below, but they are neither a preregistration nor an immutable record that rules out unarchived adjustments. 

4.6 Intervention policy 

At an alarm, the detector scans backward to its most recent nonpositive Sj and maps that window to a token position. The controller extends the rollback earlier by a fixed margin, truncates the generated suffix and key–value cache, rebuilds monitor and n-gram state, resets the detector, and enters a refractory period. It then re-decodes with lower temperature, a repetition penalty, and a list of suspect n-grams that may not be completed again. The implementation permits multiple rerolls, although the recorded trajectories have at most one alarm in the main aggregate. Unlike the earlier token-level controller, the present policy disables controller-authored correction markers. This design avoids confounding the monitor with explicit revision prompts and is consistent with evidence that quantized reasoners may already overproduce branch-opening markers during overthinking (Lotfi et al., 2026). The intervention is instead local to decoding state and sampling constraints. This choice is not yet established as optimal and remains part of the composite intervention that must be ablated. 

4.7 Computational complexity 

The controller does not add model forward passes before an intervention: it consumes logits already produced for sampling. It does add vocabulary-wide work: the current implementation separately evaluates log-softmax for entropy and again for chosen-token log-probability, in addition to the sampler’s softmax. The current n-gram tracker stores the retained trajectory and searches n-gram occurrences in each window; its worst-case cost is not optimized. Backtracking adds sampling only to the extent that abandoned tokens are replaced, but the outer generation loop also imposes a hard cap on total sampling iterations. 

# 5 Experimental Setup 

5.1 Model, precision, and task 

All central MATH results use deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B at checkpoint revision 

ad9f0ae0864d7fbcd1cd905e3c6c5b069cc8b562 , a distilled reasoning model released with DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025). The checkpoint revision was recovered from the local Hugging Face cache and predates the central MATH runs. The model was loaded through bitsandbytes in 4-bit FP4 format, with quantized values stored as uint8 , no nested quantization ( bnb_4bit_use_double_quant=False ), and FP16 computa-tion ( torch.float16 ). Experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX A4000 GPU with 16 GB of memory, using NVIDIA driver 595.84 . The host system reported CUDA 13.2 . Exact historical versions of 7PyTorch, Transformers, and bitsandbytes were not archived and cannot be reconstructed because the Python environment was rebuilt after the central artifacts were generated. MATH-500 is a 500-problem subset associated with the process-supervision evaluation of Lightman et al. (2024). The repository shuffles the test split and prompts the model to reason step by step and finish with a marked final answer. The scorer performs exact equality after custom normalization of common LaTeX forms; it is not a symbolic-equivalence checker. The present manuscript reports only MATH-500 experiments. Results from the earlier pilot are intentionally excluded rather than combined with the current protocol. 

5.2 Calibration, threshold chronology, and evaluation sets 

The MATH calibration job generated 100 examples per precision. It retained 34 correct, non-truncated INT4 trajectories containing 3,531 windows and 22 FP16 trajectories containing 2,584 windows. The FP16 threshold search exhausted its grid at approximately 10 8 while still recording a 100% in-sample alarm rate, so we do not treat the FP16 MATH controller as calibrated. Artifact timestamps place the creation of threshold_log10.json after the seed-1 automatic-threshold run and before the subsequent manual-threshold evaluation artifacts. Before this manual threshold choice, the calibration/seed-0 artifacts and the seed-1 automatic-threshold run cover 260 unique MATH-500 IDs. We construct a chronology-aware audit by excluding those IDs and retaining the chronologically first paired observation for every problem ID first encountered after the threshold file was created. Run boundaries are not treated as evaluation boundaries: the unit of chronological exclusion and deduplication is the individual problem ID. A run may contain both previously exposed and newly encountered IDs; only the latter can enter this audit, and only through their first post-threshold pair. This procedure yields the 240-pair chronology-audit set (240 unique first-post-threshold pairs), the primary evaluation set used throughout the remainder of the paper. For broader historical coverage, we also deduplicate all available seed-matched pairs in chronological priority order, yielding the 467-pair historical-coverage set (467 unique pairs). This set includes the 200 seed-1 IDs that were available before or during selection of the manual threshold and is therefore exploratory rather than an independent post-selection evaluation. File timestamps and manifests make the chronology reconstruction auditable, but they do not constitute a preregistration or prove that no unarchived tuning occurred. 

5.3 Decoding and controller configuration 

MATH jobs set a maximum of 20,000 sampling iterations. Vanilla and pre-alarm controlled decoding sample at temperature 1.0. Windows contain 64 tokens with stride 32, and the monitor tracks generated 6–8-grams. The linear score weights are 0.15 , 0.10 , 0.20 , 0.35 , 0.18 , 0.02 for entropy, negative chosen log-probability, repetition, confident repetition, positive local entropy change, and negative local entropy change, respectively. An intervention uses a 64-token pre-changepoint margin, at most three rerolls, temperature 0.6, repetition penalty 

1.1, suspect n-gram blocking, and two refractory windows. Prompt injection is disabled. The base experiment seed controls both dataset ordering and a deterministic hash used to derive an item-level sampling seed. Within every retained pair, vanilla and controlled rows share the item-level seed. Different items generally have only one observed seed; this is not a repeated-seed evaluation on a fixed item set. 

5.4 Baselines and controls 

The valid reference is vanilla stochastic sampling with the same initial temperature and outer iteration cap. The repository also contains random rollback, periodic rollback, restart, and self-correction policies evaluated on the same problem identities. Their runs use a per-ID upper bound equal to ⌊1.10 Bi⌋, where Bi is the sampled-token count of the corresponding selected MGT-B trajectory. The controls therefore receive a slightly larger permitted sampling budget than MGT-B on every item. All four control artifacts cover 500 IDs and obey these caps. Their lower realized mean token counts arise from their own intervention and termination dynamics, rather than from a tighter experimental constraint. Because the controls were generated in 8separate stochastic executions, their aggregate accuracies are interpreted as policy-level estimates over the same benchmark set rather than exact token-by-token counterfactual replays. Entropy-only, repetition-only, combined heuristics, classical CUSUM, non-reset martingale/e-process, feature knockouts, best-of-n, and self-consistency have no complete result artifacts. 

5.5 Metrics and uncertainty 

We report denominators and counts for accuracy, and paired method comparisons use the exact two-sided McNemar test. For the 240-pair chronology-audit set and the 467-pair historical-coverage set, we additionally report paired problem-level percentile bootstrap intervals. The chronology-audit set yields a 95% interval of 

[−1.25 , +6 .25] percentage points; the historical-coverage set yields [+1 .93 , +7 .07] points. These quantities describe variability across the observed problem pairs only. They do not account for manual threshold selection, possible unarchived adjustments, model selection, or missing repeated generation seeds. In particular, the nominal p = 0 .000753 for the 467-pair historical-coverage set is not treated as independent evidence for the manually selected threshold, and none of the reported analyses is described as confirmatory.

6.2 Alarms, recoveries, and unchanged trajectories 

Within the exploratory 467-pair historical-coverage set, 151 trajectories alarm (32.33%). Among them, 29 are corrections and 8 are regressions; equivalently, 19.21% of alarmed trajectories recover a wrong vanilla final answer and 5.30% lose a correct one. All 316 no-alarm controlled token sequences are exactly identical to vanilla. This establishes a software-level selectivity property: output changes occur only when the controller activates. It does not establish detector precision. Without a time-local label, a correction after an alarm is evidence that the joint monitor–intervention pipeline helped, not proof that the alarm localized the original mistake. 9The median absolute alarm position is 1,928 tokens and the median rollback length is 640 tokens in this aggregate. Neither quantity is detection delay: no true changepoint is annotated.

7.3 Ablations for component attribution 

The random and periodic rollback controls already provide evidence that the complete monitoring-guided policy is more effective than the uninformed intervention schedules evaluated here. They therefore address whether selective intervention matters at the policy level. They do not, however, determine which part of MGT-B produces this advantage. The code contains configuration scaffolding for feature knockouts and alternative monitoring modes, but no complete primary result artifacts for these variants. We consequently cannot estimate the marginal contribution of entropy, chosen-token log-probability, repetition, confident repetition, local entropy change, positional calibration, reset accumulation, adaptive rollback, lower temperature, repetition penalty, or suspect n-gram blocking. These remaining ablations are intended to attribute the observed advantage across components, not to establish from scratch whether monitoring-guided intervention outperforms the uninformed controls. Final correctness and non-truncation are also weak proxies for a locally healthy reasoning path. Independent reference and threshold-validation sets are necessary. 

Empirical scope, chronology, and generalization. The 240-pair chronology-audit set is the most relevant available audit of temporal separation from the manual threshold choice, but it is reconstructed retrospectively from file timestamps, manifests, and surviving artifacts. It is neither preregistered nor an immutable guarantee that no unarchived adjustment occurred. Its exclusion and deduplication rules operate at the individual problem-ID level; the composition or filename of the run containing an observation does not create a separate evaluation stratum. The 467-pair historical-coverage set includes 200 seed-1 IDs available before or during threshold selection; its nominal p-value and bootstrap interval therefore omit selection uncertainty and are not interpreted as independent significance. The central evidence concerns one 1.5B distilled model loaded in 4-bit precision on MATH-500. No second model family, clean full-precision condition, repeated-seed matrix, or disjoint dataset currently supports broader generalization. The paper therefore studies one quantized mathematical-reasoning setting, not small language models in general, and none of the reported MATH-500 analyses is described as confirmatory. 

Monitoring-guided selectivity is supported, but its components remain coupled. No temporal degradation labels or true changepoints exist, so alarm precision/recall, an independently measured false-alarm rate, and detection delay are unavailable. Final-answer recoveries therefore characterize the joint monitoring-and-intervention pipeline rather than the semantic correctness of individual alarms. Nevertheless, the random and periodic rollback controls provide evidence for monitoring-guided selectivity at the policy level: both are evaluated under per-item sampling caps 10% above the corresponding MGT-B cost, yet neither reproduces the aggregate improvement obtained by MGT-B. This makes backtracking alone or an uninformed 12 intervention schedule unlikely explanations for the observed gain. The remaining limitation is component attribution. Rollback localization, lower temperature, repetition penalty, and suspect n-gram blocking change together after an alarm, while the feature set and CUSUM-shaped accumulation are also evaluated only as a combined monitor. The current experiments therefore support the value of the complete monitoring-guided policy, but do not determine which monitoring or repair component is responsible for its advantage. The positive within-run self-correction effect further confirms that generic revision is a substantive alternative, motivating direct component and policy ablations. 

Evolution beyond the prior controller is not yet isolated. The present architecture differs substantially from the earlier token-level preprint, but the older controller has not yet been rerun on the same MATH-500 items, seeds, and evaluation protocol. The current study therefore cannot attribute any difference to windowing, positional calibration, mixture betting, adaptive rollback, or marker-free re-decoding individually. A direct historical-version baseline and a small set of component ablations are required to establish which revisions matter. 

Measurement, cost, and reproducibility. Exact normalization can misclassify mathematically equivalent or malformed expressions, and discordant cases have not been blindly adjudicated with an official or symbolic scorer. The runs were executed on a single NVIDIA RTX A4000 GPU with 16 GB of memory and NVIDIA driver 595.84 ; the same host was documented with system CUDA 13.2 . Exact historical versions of PyTorch, Transformers, and bitsandbytes were not archived and cannot be reconstructed because the Python environment was rebuilt after the central artifacts were generated. The currently installed package versions are therefore not attributed retrospectively to these runs. Energy consumption, peak memory, CPU overhead, system load, and complete run-time conditions were not recorded consistently, so logged latency is treated as descriptive rather than as a controlled systems benchmark. Cache cropping is validated against the repository’s software fixtures, but not across every supported Transformers cache implementation. 

# 9 Conclusion 

This work develops the monitoring idea from an earlier token-level controller into a window-based, positionally calibrated, CUSUM-shaped system for quantized small-model reasoning. The design is motivated by a simple negative result: centered chosen-token log-probability is a valid self-consistency martingale under the model’s own sampling law, but is not by itself a health signal and can remain silent during confident repetition. The revised controller therefore combines uncertainty with direct degeneration features, estimates where to roll back, restores a coherent token and KV-cache prefix together with monitor state, and targets subsequent sampling through constrained re-decoding. The 240-pair chronology-audit set, which includes only IDs first observed after the manual threshold choice, changes accuracy from 82/240 to 88/240, a positive but statistically uncertain difference of 2.50 points. This audit is defined at the problem-ID level and is unaffected by the run boundary in which a retained pair was generated. The broader 467-pair historical-coverage set yields a larger 4.50-point difference, but it includes seed-1 IDs exposed before or during threshold selection and is therefore not treated as independent evidence for h = 10 . Operational diagnostics on that set show that non-alarmed outputs remain unchanged and that alarmed trajectories contain more corrections than regressions, while explicit accounting separates retained tokens from abandoned sampling. These observations establish a concrete selective monitoring-and-repair behavior for one model, precision, and task, not a validated detector, a confirmatory accuracy result, or a general reasoning improvement. Among the evaluated intervention policies on the exploratory 467-pair historical-coverage set, MGT-B attains the highest aggregate accuracy even though each control is permitted a per-item budget 10% above the corresponding MGT-B sampled cost. The most important next tests are a prospectively fixed threshold with disjoint evaluation, a direct comparison with the prior token-level controller, monitor and intervention ablations, degradation labels or controlled changepoints, and broader model and dataset coverage. Those 13 experiments can determine whether the observed direction replicates independently and which parts of the sequential monitor and targeted rollback produce it.

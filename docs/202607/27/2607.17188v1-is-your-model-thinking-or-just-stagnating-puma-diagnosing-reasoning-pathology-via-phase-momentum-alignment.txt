## Abstract 

Test-time scaling empowers Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to tackle complex tasks via extensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT). How-ever, this often induces the "overthinking" paradox, where redun-dant reasoning increases computational overhead without guaran-teeing accuracy. Existing test-time efficiency optimization methods primarily fall into two categories: information-theoretic approaches, which are prone to "deceptive convergence" where low uncertainty masks hallucinations, and latent representation analyses, which are often post-hoc, lacking the real-time sensitivity for dynamic reasoning. To bridge this gap, we first posit the Phase-Momentum Alignment Hypothesis , asserting that reasoning correctness hinges on the temporal synchronization between geometric momentum and uncertainty resolution. We then theoretically formulate the 

Cognitive-Energy Model to characterize these dynamics through two orthogonal dimensions: Geometric Cognitive Effort (quantified by latent velocity and tortuosity) and Entropic Cognitive Uncertainty .To operationalize this, we introduce PUMA (Phase-Uncertainty 

Momentum Alignment), a training-free framework employing a tiered diagnostic architecture. By coupling lightweight phase moni-toring with event-triggered geometric analysis, PUMA effectively distinguishes active exploration from passive stagnation, enabling precise interventions through adaptive truncation or corrective measures. Extensive experiments on LRMs spanning 1.5B to 32B demonstrate that PUMA consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across diverse benchmarks, achieving a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off and robust cross-domain generalization.

## 1 Introduction 

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), such as OpenAI o1 [ 16 ] and DeepSeek-R1 [ 13 ], have achieved remarkable success in complex reasoning tasks. To further enhance these capabilities, recent ap-proaches leverage test-time scaling , allocating more inference-time computation to generate extensive Chain-of-Thought sequences [ 42 ]. arXiv:2607.17188v1 [cs.AI] 19 Jul 2026 Yan et al. 

While this extended generation enables complex reasoning, it also introduces the risk of "overthinking", characterized by redundant and repetitive reasoning that escalate computational costs without guaranteeing accuracy [ 11 , 33 ]. Consequently, developing mech-anisms for efficient reasoning , particularly through dynamic self-evaluation, has become a key priority to optimize the trade-off between computational cost and reasoning reliability. Current efforts optimizing test-time computation generally fall into two primary streams: information-theoretic approaches and latent space representation analysis. From an information-theoretic perspective, methods like Think or Not [ 47 ] propose an Adaptive Think strategy that dynamically halts generation upon detecting low-entropy states, empirically validating that effective reasoning typically manifests as a progressive transition from high initial uncertainty to low-entropy certainty. However, observations in 

Entropy-Lens [ 2] indicate that relying solely on entropy introduces significant ambiguity since high entropy may signify effective ex-ploration rather than ineffective exploration while low entropy can deceptively mask "confident hallucinations," rendering it an unreliable proxy for correctness. In parallel, approaches grounded in latent representation analysis, such as Tracing the Traces [38 ], evaluate reasoning quality by monitoring the temporal evolution of hidden states, demonstrating that the internal representational dynamics inherently encode signals predictive of output correct-ness. Nevertheless, these methods primarily operate in a post-hoc manner which renders them unsuitable for real-time monitoring. Although CoRE [ 21 ] attempts to address this by identifying lo-cal cyclical loops, it remains inefficient at detecting subtle error patterns emerging in the early stages of reasoning. In summary, neither paradigm alone suffices: entropy signals are prone to decep-tive convergence patterns stemming from the intrinsic ambiguity of entropy, while latent analysis often lacks real-time sensitivity. To bridge this gap, we draw inspiration from human cognitive processes, specifically the dynamic interplay between metacogni-tive monitoring and cognitive effort . Human reasoning typically evolves through structured phases from initial problem understand-ing and divergence to iteration and final convergence. Crucially, this mental effort is non-uniform across phases: intense exploration requires high cognitive load, while convergence is characterized by a reduction of effort. Hypothesizing that this phenomenon of phase-dependent thinking effort is also inherent in the generation dynamics of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), we seek to decode these internal signals. To validate this intuition, we analyze the joint evolution of latent representation dynamics and entropy shifts. Our empirical observations, as visualized in Figure 1, reveal two distinct thinking patterns. In solvable cases (Figure 1(a)), the model exhibits synchronized dynamics : a sharp reduction in entropic un-certainty is strictly coupled with high latent momentum, signaling a distinct transition from exploratory divergence to structural con-vergence. Conversely, unsolvable or incorrect cases (Figure 1(b)) manifest a significant asynchrony : persistent high entropy coincides with vanishing latent momentum, reflecting a state of "cognitive stagnation" where the model continues to generate tokens despite having ceased constructive semantic processing. Based on these insights, we formally propose the Phase-Momentum Alignment Hy-pothesis : The correctness of a reasoning trajectory is closely correlated with the temporal synchronization between its epistemic uncertainty (phase) and its internal semantic Effort (momentum). 

Motivated by these insights, we formally define the Cognitive-Energy Model , which decomposes the reasoning process into two orthogonal dimensions: (1) Geometric Cognitive Effort , quantifying the geometric dynamics of the latent trajectory via Global Thinking Intensity (tortuosity) and Local Semantic Gain (velocity); and (2) 

Entropic Cognitive Uncertainty , leveraging lookahead entropy as a proxy for the solution-space phase. By synthesizing these met-rics, we derive the Net Reasoning Momentum to operationalize the 

Phase-Momentum Alignment Hypothesis for diagnosis of reason-ing pathology. To implement this model, we introduce PUMA, a training-free framework for adaptive cognitive control. It employs a tiered architecture where a lightweight entropy-based Finite State Machine (FSM) serves as a continuous monitor to track phase tran-sitions with minimal computational overhead. The system triggers the Geometric Cognitive Effort calculation strictly upon detect-ing critical shifts to jointly diagnose the reasoning pattern. Based on the diagnostic outcomes, PUMA executes extensible interven-tion policies, such as effectively truncating generation or applying corrective measures to ensure robust reasoning integrity. Our contributions are summarized as follows: (1) We formulate the Cognitive-Energy Model and the Phase-Momentum Alignment Hypothesis . By introducing Net Reasoning Momentum derived from 

Global Thinking Intensity and Local Semantic Gain , we provide a formal interpretation interpretation of "thinking effort" that effec-tively distinguishes valid reasoning from cognitive stagnation. (2) To operationalize this, we propose PUMA, a training-free inference framework that integrates a lightweight entropy-based FSM with event-triggered cognitive diagnosis, enabling adaptive supervision of the reasoning process without parameter updates. (3) Extensive experiments validate that PUMA achieves a superior accuracy-cost trade-off. Crucially, our framework demonstrates robust general-ization across diverse models and reasoning domains, consistently outperforming distinct optimization paradigms. 

## 2 Related Work 2.1 Adaptive and Efficient Reasoning 

To mitigate the “overthinking” phenomenon and computational overhead in LRMs, recent research focuses on optimizing reason-ing length and eliminating redundant computation [ 11 , 26 , 33 , 39 ]. We categorize these efforts into two primary streams. One major category is Training-based methods, which internalize efficiency via parameter updates, primarily enhancing performance through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on compressed CoT traces [ 18 , 23 ,25 , 30 , 44 ], Reinforcement Learning (RL) with length-aware penal-ties [ 1, 3, 34 , 46 , 49 ], or implicit reasoning within continuous latent spaces [ 29 , 31 , 32 , 45 ]. Alternatively, Training-free methods offer a model-agnostic approach, optimizing inference through prompt constraints for brevity [ 5, 14 , 20 , 24 ] or dynamic interventions such as early exiting and speculative decoding [4, 22, 43, 47].

## 2.3 Latent Representation Analysis. 

Previous studies have shown that probing the hidden states of Large Language Models (LLMs) reveals informative signals regarding model reliability [ 48 ], safety [ 37 ], and generation performance [ 41 ]. Building on these insights, recent works have attempted to utilize latent space dynamics to enhance the efficiency of reasoning tasks. Chain-of-Embedding (CoE) [ 41 ] estimates response correctness by analyzing the geometric trajectory of hidden states. However, CoE depends on the relative displacement between the input and final output states for normalization, rendering it unsuitable for real-time step-level verification. [ 21 ] utilizes geometric analysis of latent trajectories to identify redundant patterns in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation. Latent-Trajectory signals [ 38 ] characterize the temporal evolution of representations, such as cumulative change, to guide sample selection. However, these metrics rely on coarse-grained segment averaging, which fails to pinpoint fine-grained logical inconsistencies within individual steps.

## 5 PUMA: Adaptive Cognitive Control 

To operationalize the Cognitive-Energy Model defined in Section 4, we propose a training-free framework, as shown in Figure 2), in-tegrating lightweight monitoring, event-triggered cognitive diag-nosis, and extensible dynamic intervention. The system orches-trates the inference process through three stages: (1) A lightweight entropy-based Finite State Machine (FSM) monitors phase transi-tions in real-time; (2) Upon detecting critical phase shifts, the sys-tem triggers the Geometric Cognitive Effort calculation to jointly diagnose the reasoning pattern; (3) Based on the diagnosis, the system adaptively executes corresponding intervention measures, effectively truncating generation or applying corrective measures. 

## 5.1 Entropy-based FSM Monitoring 

To capture valid reasoning signals, we initiate monitoring strictly after the Problem Understanding phase PUnd (as defined in Sec-tion 4.2). We formalize the FSM over a ternary state space S =

{P Conv , PDiv , PUndet }. where any interval failing to satisfy the strict thresholds for either convergence or divergence is categorized as the indeterminate state PUndet . To ensure robust detection, the sys-tem tracks phase stability using a sliding window of size 𝑤 fixed 

according to Eq. 11. To balance diagnostic precision with runtime efficiency, we em-ploy an event-driven protocol. The Diagnostic Trigger is activated strictly upon the detection of critical state transitions: ❶ Con-vergence Alert: The entropy consistently drops ( 𝑠 𝑡 → P Conv ), signaling a potential solution candidate. ❷ Divergence Persis-tence Alert: The entropy persists in high-uncertainty ( 𝑠 𝑡 ∈ P Div ), suggesting potential infinite looping or cognitive stagnation. Once triggered, the system computes the Net Reasoning Momentum Φ𝑡 

(Eq. 8) and synthesizes it with the current phase state to identify the underlying cognitive pattern as defined in Eq. 12.

By executing TRUNCATE success at the exact mo-ment the model satisfies healthy convergence (low entropy coupled with relaxed momentum), we guarantee that the solution fixed point 𝑦 ∗ has been reached. Crucially, this preemptive intervention implicitly manages Post-Convergence Repetition ( SRedundant ) by eliminating the temporal window required for the model to drift into a redundant state, thereby maximizing efficiency without a separate suppression mechanism. 

Cognitive Circuit Breaking & Restart (Targeting SStagnant ). 

To prevent hallucinations induced by reasoning failure, we em-ploy a "Circuit Breaker" mechanism. When the diagnosis identifies 

Energy-Entropy Misalignment (𝑆 𝑡 = SStagnant ), the system executes TRUNCATE fail . This action halts the generation of vacuous tokens where the model has "given up" (vanishing momentum) despite un-resolved ambiguity. This dynamic intervention specifically targets the pathology of "reasoning collapse," ensuring computational re-sources are not wasted on logically invalid content and preventing error propagation at an early stage. Furthermore, Circuit Breaker activation signals critical cogni-tive barriers, likely stemming from missing domain information or intrinsic reasoning insufficiency. We preliminarily explore a 

Retrieval-Augmented Restart mechanism on mathematical datasets, where external context (e.g., theorems) is injected to reset the rea-soning trajectory and rectify potential hallucinations. Given the high extensibility of our intervention mechanism, we reserve com-prehensive evaluation of such advanced strategies for future work. 

## 6 Experiments 6.1 Experimental Setup 

Models and Datasets. We evaluate PUMA on four Large Reason-ing Models (LRMs) spanning parameter scales from 1.5B to 32B. Specifically, we employ the DeepSeek-R1-Distill [13 ] series (1.5B, 7B, 14B) and QwQ-32B [35 ]. To ensure a comprehensive evaluation, we select five benchmarks across three domains: Mathematical Reasoning (GSM8k [ 8 ], MATH-500 [ 15 ], and AIME 2025), Scien-tific Reasoning (GPQA-Diamond [ 27 ]), and General Complex Reasoning (MMLU-Pro [40]). 

Baselines. We benchmark against representative efficiency opti-mization methods across three distinct paradigms: 

• Information-Theoretic Methods: Think or Not [47] adopts an entropy-based halting mechanism, utilizing step-level entropy shifts and low-uncertainty thresholds to adaptively truncate generation when the model exhibits sufficient confidence. 

• Latent Representation Analysis: Tracing the Traces (LT) [ 38 ]leverages Latent-Trajectory Signals (e.g., cumulative trajectory changes) to enable the early selection of high-quality traces, estimating the correctness of reasoning chains in a label-free manner. 

• Training-based Control: AdaptThink [49 ] employs a multi-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework to train a policy that dynamically switches between elaborate reasoning and di-rect answering. Due to the substantial computational overhead of RL training, we restrict the comparison of this baseline to the 1.5B and 7B parameter scales. 

Variants for Hypothesis Verification. To explicitly verify the Phase-Momentum Alignment and disentangle the contributions of information-theoretic and geometric signals, we construct two ablated variants of our framework: 

• PUMA entropy : This variant relies solely on the Information-Theo-retic dimension, executing truncation strictly based on the low-entropy convergence signal derived from our refined Lookahead Entropy (Section 4.2), ignoring geometric momentum. 

• PUMA latent : This variant operates exclusively on the geometric analysis of hidden states, triggering truncation solely when the Net Reasoning Momentum drops below the relaxation threshold (i.e., Φ𝑡 < 𝜖 ), without considering phase alignment. 

Implementation Details. We perform all experiments on NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPUs. For Entropic Cognitive Dispersion , we employ DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B as the default proxy model 𝑀 𝜙 .To balance monitoring efficiency and accuracy, we define the rea-soning step granularity as every three newline separators (‘ \n\n ’). Regarding hyperparameters, we set the phase sensitivity 𝛼 = 0.05 

and momentum threshold 𝜖 = 0.1 with a sliding window 𝑤 = 3; sen-sitivity analyses for these parameters and the proxy model size are provided in Section 6.4. For Geometric Cognitive Effort , we analyze the hidden states of the final 25% of layers using Angular Distance (𝛿 𝑎𝑛𝑔 ). All reported results are averaged over three independent runs to mitigate generation randomness.

## 6.3 Ablation and In-Depth Analysis 

Ablation Study. Table 1 demonstrates that PUMA significantly outperforms its decoupled variants, confirming the necessity of the Phase-Momentum Alignment. For instance, on the MATH-500 benchmark with the 32B model, PUMA entropy and PUMA latent 

achieve accuracies of 85.40% and 84.20%, respectively. In contrast, the full PUMA effectively synergizes these signals to reach 91.40%, achieving a substantial gain of over 6% compared to the single-modal baselines. To further analyze the source of this improvement, we define four reasoning quadrants formed by two orthogonal di-mensions: the correctness of the model’s original reasoning ( 𝑦 𝑜𝑟𝑖 )and the correctness after intervention ( 𝑦 𝑡𝑟𝑢𝑛𝑐 ), these quadrants are categorized as: (1) Efficiency Gain (𝑦 𝑜𝑟𝑖 ✓, 𝑦 𝑡𝑟𝑢𝑛𝑐 ✓), where trunca-tion preserves correctness; (2) Resource Saving (𝑦 𝑜𝑟𝑖 ×, 𝑦 𝑡𝑟𝑢𝑛𝑐 ×), where tokens are saved on inevitable errors; (3) Accuracy Gain 

(𝑦 𝑜𝑟𝑖 ×, 𝑦 𝑡𝑟𝑢𝑛𝑐 ✓), representing rectified truncation from overthink-ing; and (4) Accuracy Loss (𝑦 𝑜𝑟𝑖 ✓, 𝑦 𝑡𝑟𝑢𝑛𝑐 ×), denoting premature failure. As illustrated in Figure 3(a). Empirically, while PUMA entropy 

and PUMA latent achieve 85.4% and 84.2% Efficiency Gain respec-tively, they suffer from 2.0% and 4.0% Accuracy Loss. In contrast, PUMA achieves the highest Efficiency Gain (87.8%) with 0% Accu-racy Loss and a notable 3.6% Accuracy Gain. 

Fine-grained Mechanism Analysis. The performance disparity stems from the intrinsic limitations of uncoupled diagnostic signals. First, PUMA latent exhibits significant Accuracy Loss (4.0%) due to the sensitivity dilemma of the momentum threshold 𝜖 : a loose 𝜖 

triggers premature truncation before semantic convergence, sever-ing valid reasoning paths essential for correct deduction, whereas a strictly tight 𝜖 fails to arrest "overthinking" in time. Second, PUMA entropy suffers from non-negligible loss (2.0%) as low en-tropy can be deceptive, , thereby masking "confident hallucinations" where the model stabilizes superficially despite unsettled internal momentum. In contrast, the full PUMA resolves these trade-offs by subjecting 𝜖 to Phase constraints . By functionally inhibiting trun-cation during active exploration (even if momentum fluctuates), PUMA acts as a semantic gatekeeper that guarantees intervention only upon dual-signal synchronization between geometric momen-tum and entropic phase. Consequently, this alignment eliminates premature interventions (0% Accuracy Loss) while enabling the system to precisely rectify overthinking errors exactly when a valid intermediate thought is formed.

## 7 Conclusion 

In this work, we first posit the Phase-Momentum Alignment Hypoth-esis , identifying that valid reasoning hinges on the temporal syn-chronization between entropic uncertainty and geometric cognitive effort. We rigorously formalize this insight via the Cognitive-Energy Model to precisely distinguish active exploration from passive stag-nation. To operationalize this, we propose PUMA, a training-free framework that optimizes test-time reasoning through dynamic diagnosis and intervention mechanism. Extensive experiments demonstrate that PUMA consistently outperforms representative baselines in information-theoretic, latent-based, and training-based paradigms, resulting in a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off and robust cross-domain generalization. By establishing a transparent metric for reasoning integrity, our approach offers a robust and theoretically principled path for reliable inference scaling. Yan et al.
